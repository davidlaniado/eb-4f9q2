What to build for DirectMax, in order

    Rules baked in: no ingredient names, no "arousal", no hard/firm, no white coat (black/maroon scrubs), no all-animation, brand-agnostic where marked, Cashvertising 3-5 per script, script pipeline for anything over 30 seconds.

  1. "The part nobody explains" long copy, 6 cheap videos. Primary text = the song's mechanism as a 1,200-word first-person story (hotel lot → "long week" → tried three → keep vs start). Videos: (a) car-selfie actor, (b) AI nurse black scrubs over b-roll, (c) cartoon brain + spine signal as b-roll inside the presenter, (d) Pixar still thumbnail on live video, (e) text-scroll over the nightstand book, (f) 10-s product counter. One copy, six ads. Noverly model. **Build first.**
  2. Mundane-photo letter static (Auniva). Image: two coffee mugs on a hallway floor / a hotel parking lot at night / the book on the nightstand. No product. Primary text = the letter from #1. This is the split-image long-copy crack. Three photos × one letter = three ads.
  3. Text-scroll video (Auniva). Same letter, scrolling over one still, 2:30, no voice, no music. Cheapest video we will ever make.
  4. Three symptoms, one signal (Aurivita/UltimaPeak). "Can't start. Can't keep. Can't stop thinking about it. Three problems? One signal that isn't getting through." Static + 30-s video.

  6. Pharmacist confession (Primal Storm). Counter, black scrubs. "I've filled the blue pill for twenty years. In June my own script came across my counter. I didn't fill it." → keep vs start.

  10. Walmart aisle rating (Noverly nurse). Walk the men's-health aisle, 0/10 → 10/10 → "1000/10: the one that isn't on a shelf." Risk: naming shelf products; use generic bottles.
  11. Statics pack: "8 signs it's in your head (men over 40)" icon grid; "It takes 2 minutes" (the visit); iMessage from her "babe… what changed?"; "She asked for round two" comic; before/after sketch (Friday 9pm → Sunday 9am); shock image = the three old pills in the trash.
  12. Pixar narrated story (David's pick; UltimaPeak `top/02.mp4` and Primal Storm `top/03.mp4` are the reference cuts). Build with the song story. Flag: all-animation was rejected on DirectMax 10/5; test one with a live cold open + Pixar body.

    13. Parked (their format, not ours): podcast/studio clips (Meta rejects on our accounts), owner-in-warehouse (no owner), "extra 2 inches" claims, health-outcome claims (heart attack), ingredient grids.

What the winners have in common

  1. One long primary text, many cheap visuals. Noverly's #1 ad (672 copies, 5 pages) is a 2,000-word "Your testosterone came back normal. That's the part nobody explains to you" story. The SAME text runs with a cartoon-organ explainer, a LEGO explainer, a Pixar skeleton, a nurse in the Walmart aisle, a car selfie and an AI b-roll montage. The copy is the asset; the video is a thumbnail. Every brand does a version of this.
  2. "Here's what nobody tells you" grammar. Noverly, Aurivita (×6), Primal Storm, UltimaPeak all open with it. Our chorus already has it ("the part nobody fixed").
  3. Three symptoms, one root cause. Aurivita: weak stream + weaker erections + up 4×/night = one cause. UltimaPeak: soft + fast + no drive = "supply problem, and supply is restorable."
  4. Anti-pitch opener. "Don't buy Aurivita. Read this first." "Please don't buy it at Walmart. I did." "F*ck this product" (review-turned-testimonial). "Okay don't buy this yet. Just listen."
  5. Insider confession. Pharmacist who refused to fill his own Viagra script (28 yrs); urologist "sick of men saying L-arginine fixes ED"; county coroner "opened 4,000 chests"; concierge doc to 110 adult-film performers; paramedic "70% of men who had a heart attack had ED first" (health claim, not for us).
  6. Woman POV. "My sister told me her husband was hard again in two weeks. I ordered it that same day." "I stopped reaching for my diabetic husband two years ago." "He went three rounds and I had to tell him to stop." Aurivita's Lucy Thorne page: "If your man goes from hard to soft the second you switch positions…"
  7. Mundane photo + long letter. Auniva (34 copies, 11 days, 8 pages): a photo of a workbench / a Singer sewing machine / a bass boat / a plate of steak, and a 1,500-word first-person letter in the primary text. No product in the image. Rosabella does the same with parables ("Call him Walt") over garden rows, a police car through a window, soup bowls. Leg Cramp Reset's split image is the same family.
  8. Text-scroll video. Auniva's whole letter scrolling over a still of eggs and bacon. ~3 minutes. No voice. The cheapest video in the set, and it's in their top 3.
  9. Presenter containers. Nurse in scrubs answering a comment bubble (Aurivita, Noverly). Woman holding the pouch, one-word yellow captions (UltimaPeak). Owner in the warehouse with factory b-roll (Primal Storm). Older woman comparing two labels, "this is why it's not working" (Rosabella). Amish farmer in a barn (Rosabella). Nurse walking the Walmart aisle rating bottles 0/10 → 1000/10 (Noverly).
  10. Animation, four flavors: cartoon organ characters (heart in ICU bed), Pixar couple story ("his wife noticed before he did"), LEGO explainer, product-jar superhero. **Supplements get away with 100% animation. We got rejected on DirectMax 10/5 for it.** Our version = animation as b-roll inside a live presenter, or a Pixar still as the thumbnail on a live video.
  11. Caption styles: one WORD at a time centered (most common), one short line in a white box, yellow-highlighted keyword in a sentence.
  12. Offers: buy 2 get 1/2 free + "6 hours left", "70% off, 3 for $33", 60-day money back, free second bottle, "we pack two paid and two free" shown in the warehouse. Rx can't bundle; our equivalents are per-dose price, 2-day plain box, money-back.
  13. Statics that repeat: "Don't take these unless you want…" + 5 checkmarks; "8 signs you're losing it in bed, men over 45" icon grid; "It takes 3 seconds to read this"; iMessage from the wife ("Babe… it's only been 9 days"); before/after sketch; shock medical photo (artery slices mild→severe, kidney X-ray); "WE'RE SO SORRY" apology letter; comic-book "She asked for a rematch"; ruler + tiny crumb innuendo; Ozempic squirrel cartoon.
